Power Amplifiers - Introduction

Definition (From: power amp int.jpeg)

Power Amplifier: It is an amplifier with high output power.

Main Purpose: It is mainly used to deliver a large amount of power to a load.

Output Form: The output may be in the form of large voltage or large current.

Alternate Name: It is also called as large signal amplifier.

Class A Power Amplifier

From: class a power.png

Conduction Angle: 360° conduction

Operation: In a class A Amplifier, the transistor is 'on' for the full cycle (360°) of the AC input signal.

Q-point: Q-point is always positioned at the midpoint of the load line.

Fig 2: Class A Amplifier - Full 360° Conduction

Key Characteristics of Class A Amplifier

  • Transistor conducts for entire input cycle (360°)
  • Q-point is at midpoint of load line
  • Continuous conduction throughout the cycle
  • Used where linearity is most important

Applications of Power Amplifiers

From: appli of pa.png

  • It is widely used in public address system (loud speaker)
  • In radio receivers
  • TV receivers
  • Cathode Ray Tubes, etc.

Features of Power Amplifier

From: features of pa.png

  • Low output resistance
