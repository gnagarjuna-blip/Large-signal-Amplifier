Introduction to Power Amplifiers

Definition & Characteristics

Power Amplifier (Large Signal Amplifier) - An electronic device designed to increase the power of a signal. It delivers a large amount of power to the load (speaker, antenna, motor, etc.)

Fig 1: Basic Power Amplifier Block Diagram

Key Features (From: features of pa.png)

  • The power handling capacity is large
  • Low output resistance
  • Impedance matching is required for maximum power transfer
  • Power transistors with heat sink are used
  • Distortion analysis is critical

Class A Power Amplifier

360° Conduction Angle (From: class a power.png)

The transistor conducts for the entire 360° of the input cycle. The Q-point is positioned exactly at the midpoint of the load line for maximum symmetrical swing.

Fig 2: Class A Operation - Full 360° Conduction with Q-point at Center

Characteristics & Specifications

  • Conduction Angle: 360° (full cycle)
  • Efficiency:
    • Maximum 25% (Direct Coupled)
    • Maximum 50% (Transformer Coupled)
  • Distortion: Lowest among all classes
  • Biasing: Always in active region
  • Linearity: Excellent
  • Applications: Hi-Fi audio pre-amplifiers, instrumentation

Class B Power Amplifier

180° Conduction Angle

Each transistor conducts for only 180° (half cycle) of the input signal. Used in Push-Pull configuration for complete waveform reproduction.

Fig 4: Class B Operation - 180° Conduction per Transistor with Crossover Zone

Crossover Distortion

Problem: Transistors require ~0.7V to turn on. During zero-crossing, neither transistor conducts, creating distortion.

Solution: Class AB biasing (slightly forward-biasing both transistors).

Class AB Power Amplifier

The Perfect Compromise

Conduction angle is slightly more than 180° but less than 360° (typically 200°-270°). Eliminates crossover distortion while maintaining good efficiency.

Fig 6: Class AB Operation - Overlap Eliminates Crossover Distortion

Why Class AB Dominates Audio Applications

  • Efficiency: 50-70% (excellent for audio)
  • Distortion: Very low, no crossover distortion
  • Heat Dissipation: Moderate (smaller heat sinks than Class A)
  • Linearity: Excellent across entire audio range
  • Applications: Most modern audio amplifiers, car audio, home theater, studio monitors

Class C Power Amplifier

High Efficiency Specialist

Conduction angle is less than 180° (typically 120°-150°). Produces high distortion but achieves the highest efficiency (80-90%).

Fig 7: Class C Operation - Short Pulses Filtered by LC Tank Circuit

Fig 8: Class C Amplifier with LC Tank Circuit

Class C Characteristics

  • Conduction Angle: < 180° (typically 120°-150°)
  • Efficiency: Highest - 80-90%
  • Distortion: Very high (unusable for audio)
  • Requires: LC tank circuit to filter harmonics
  • Biasing: Reverse biased or zero biased
  • Applications: RF transmitters, RF power amplifiers, oscillators, frequency multipliers
