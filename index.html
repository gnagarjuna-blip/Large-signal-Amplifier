Power Amplifiers: Class A to C

Prepared by G NAGARJUNA

Comprehensive technical notes on large-signal amplification.

Power amplifiers are designed to deliver high levels of power to a load, such as a loudspeaker or antenna. Unlike voltage amplifiers, they handle large currents and require impedance matching.

  • Large Signal: Operates over a significant portion of the load line.
  • Low Output Resistance: Essential for maximum power transfer.
  • Heat Dissipation: Large-scale transistors are used to manage thermal energy.

Transistors conduct for the full 360° of the input signal. The Q-point is centered on the load line.

Pdc = VCC × ICQ
Efficiency (η) = (Pac / Pdc) × 100%
  • Max Efficiency: 25% (Direct) to 50% (Transformer coupled).
  • Advantages: No crossover distortion, high linearity.
  • Disadvantages: Low efficiency, high heat loss.

Class B

Conducts for 180°. High efficiency (~78.5%) but suffers from Crossover Distortion.

Class AB

Conducts between 180° and 360°. It is the most common class for audio as it eliminates crossover distortion while staying efficient.

Class Conduction Angle Efficiency (Max) Distortion
A 360° 25-50% Lowest
B 180° 78.5% Moderate
AB 180°-360° 50-70% Low
C < 180° > 80% Highest

The positioning of the Q-point determines the amplifier class:

  • Class A: Center of Load Line.
  • Class B: At Cut-off (X-axis).
  • Class C: Below Cut-off.
